# Continuation checkpoint: affine gaps in canonical pure-phase selection

25 October 2026. Parent: 34b46770173e30ada33e50ac76cb16710ae19287.
Do not rerun the scalar convergence, positive-occupancy, or critical-density proofs.

Let C be the subcritical densities at which every canonical sequence converges
to its deterministic-density phase. Existing upper supporting-line contacts,
zero, and a finite critical endpoint belong to C. Finite canonical mass order
gives block total-variation Lipschitz continuity in density. Limits of ordered
finite-density phases remain pure: their monotone coupling has L1 coordinate
error equal to the density difference, and mixing passes by cylinder approximation.
Thus C is closed and cofinal below the critical endpoint.

For a gap (a,b) of C, canonical limits at every interior density eta are
sandwiched between the selected endpoint phases. Their random phase density R
therefore belongs to [a,b], E R=eta by the previously proved uniform
integrability, and E Q(R)=Q(eta) by phase-current identification.

If Q is not affine on [a,b], subtract its endpoint chord. Choose the largest
maximizer if there is a positive deviation, or the largest minimizer if there
is a negative deviation. This contact c is interior. Equality of first moments
makes the expected chord deviation equal its extremal value. Hence R is
supported on the contact set, all at most c, and has mean c. Consequently R=c.
Every canonical limit is the unique phase at c, contradicting the gap.

Therefore every gap in pure canonical selection is an affine interval of Q.
In particular every density at which Q is not locally affine is selected.
C has positive points arbitrarily close to zero, since otherwise Q would be
linear from zero with positive slope, contradicting Q(r)/r -> 0.

This does not prove that affine intervals are absent or settle the critical
endpoint. Existing pure phases inside an affine interval cannot simply be
assumed canonically selected. The final proof must keep that distinction.
