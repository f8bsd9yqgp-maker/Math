# Research scope and remaining obstructions

This continuation attacked phase selection after scalar convergence and the
critical-density theorem were complete. It did not repeat old finite-ring
stationary solves, simulations, or earlier exact verifiers.

The successful new step is to use **lower** supporting contacts on a compact
density interval already bounded by a selected phase. Previously proved
uniform integrability now supplies equality of means, making minima as
useful as maxima. This yields small pure phases and the affine-gap theorem.

Several possible closure routes remain insufficient:

* Uniform integrability and equality E Q(R)=Q(eta) do not alone force R=eta.
  The new proof adds a genuine extremum and a largest-contact argument.
* Existence of a pure phase and selection of that phase by canonical rings
  are different statements. Identifying those two sets without another
  argument would falsely close the affine-gap case.
* A common-clock stationary coupling can be globally ordered with a random
  direction when its marginals mix densities. The deterministic-density
  synchronization theorem cannot simply be applied to such marginals.
* Continuity or a Lipschitz bound on Q does not exclude an exactly affine
  interval. Finite numerical curvature would not prove that all such
  intervals are absent.
* The macroscopic positive-background conclusion does not control the biased
  environment seen by a moving large pile. An erosion argument needs that
  additional control; ordinary stationary spatial averages are insufficient.
* The all-infinite initial-state construction and its coalescing infinity
  indicators were found in the existing run-six archive. They were not
  redone or claimed as new. Its unknown mean cannot be silently used to
  decide whether eta_c is finite.

Primary-source literature context was checked at
https://arxiv.org/html/1407.1668v3 (Whitehouse, Costa, Blythe and Evans,
*Maintenance of order in a moving strong condensate*). Its numerical and
approximate discussion is not an exact theorem closing the present gaps.
No external theorem was imported into this continuation, and no exhaustive
originality claim is made.

The exact audit includes illustrative probability laws showing that an affine
pile-probability curve can agree even when two-site correlations differ.
Those laws are not stationary measures for this generator and are not a
counterexample to the user's convergence problem.
